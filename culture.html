SCOTLAND, BEYOND THE POSTCARD

Culture that travels.

From Burns to ceilidhs, from Gaelic song to Hogmanay: the threads that make Scottish culture recognisable anywhere in the world.

What carries abroad

Languages: Gaelic & Scots

Scottish Gaelic is still spoken in the Western Isles, Skye and parts of the Highlands, and has a living tradition in Nova Scotia. Scots, with its own words and rhythms, is heard from Shetland to the Borders.

Robert Burns

The national poet, born in Alloway in 1759. His birthday, 25 January, is marked by Burns suppers on every continent, with haggis, an address and an Ode.

Music & dance

Bagpipes and pipe bands, fiddle and accordion, the ceilidh and Scottish country dancing: music is how many Scots stay close to home.

Literature & ideas

Sir Walter Scott, Robert Louis Stevenson and Arthur Conan Doyle, and the thinkers of the Scottish Enlightenment. Edinburgh was the first UNESCO City of Literature.

Tartan & dress

Tartan, the kilt and the sgian-dubh are worn at weddings and games all over the world; many families have their own tartan.

Scotland abroad

Culture kept alive a generation on

Caledonian societies, St Andrew's societies, pipe bands and Burns clubs have kept Scottish identity alive abroad for generations, from Nova Scotia and Ontario to the American South, Australia and New Zealand.
